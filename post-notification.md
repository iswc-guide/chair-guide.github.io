## Introduction

The track chairs are primarily responsible for the **Submission, Review and Notification phase**, with the *Research Track Chairs* overseeing the whole process. However, it is a good idea to monitor this and ensure that the process is "on track" in a timely manner. It is also important to ensure that the *Web Presence and Publicity Chairs* advertise regularly and to as diverse an audience as possible, to maximise submission numbers.

Throughout the review process, coordinate with the *Research Track Chairs* to assess submission numbers, targets for acceptance rates (these can affect the overall conference rating) and inclusion criteria (important if soliciting in related areas, such as the inclusion of "Semantic Web Agents" in ISWC 2025) to maintain awareness of possible numbers and the financial implications.

For the **mechanics** of the review and decision process — the SPC, the SPC meeting, PC management — see the [PC & SPC page](pc-and-spc.html). For **registration pricing, the venue, social events and free/reduced registrations**, see the [Local Organisers page](local-organiser.html). For **proceedings preparation** see the [Proceedings page](proceedings.html). This page focuses on what the *General Chair* needs to do **between notification and the lead-up to the event**.

---

## Notification

When sending out **notifications** for the main tracks (Research / Resources / In-Use), encourage the track chairs to advertise upcoming submission opportunities at the conference, such as Poster and Demo, late-breaking results, and Workshop submissions. This should allow revised submission of (otherwise rejected) papers, or demo submissions to accompany those papers that are accepted.  Details of submission opportunities can be included in the notification emails.

Be mindful that some Universities may restrict funding to students/academics that have a paper in the main tracks.  Therefore, submissions to the later tracks (e.g. Poster and Demo) may be affected by the results of the notifications and the attendees' availability of funding.

## Registration timing

During the notification phase, finalise the details for the registration and **publish this at the same time as releasing the notifications**; this is normally done through coordination with the *Local Chairs*. Use the predicted number of papers to estimate attendees, sponsorship, and budget to review the registration prices accordingly. Coordinate with the *Student Grants Chairs* to ensure that details regarding student grants are also published. Provide sufficient detail to allow attendees to budget their trip when notifications are released.

Travel/hotel/visa logistics are owned by the *Local Chairs* — see the [Local Organisers page](local-organiser.html).

Finalise which members of the *Organising Committee* will be attending and confirm the complimentary-registration list with the *Local Chairs*.

### Student Travel Grants

Around the time of *Main Track / DC* notifications, check in with the *Student Grants Chairs* to see how things are going. The first call for student travel grants should go out to **coincide with the notifications**.  Better still, the first call can go out **prior to submissions**, to let prospective authors know there are funds available if accepted.

## Recording

During this time, you may also be contacted by **Videolectures** (which SWSA tends to prefer) regarding recordings, flights, etc., which should have been factored into the budget by the *Local Chairs*. This may require negotiation in terms of number of days, the number of parallel sessions to record, number of personnel, etc., all of which will be affected by the finalised programme. Aim to record at least Main Track presentations, Keynotes and the Panel.

> **TIP**: Be ready to **negotiate**. Videolectures may request several days before/after the conference and a large team.

## Planning the Overall Schedule

A couple of weeks after notifications — once the *Research Track Chairs* have had a bit of time to breathe — is a good time to start to think about the **overall schedule**.  This is one of the *General Chair*'s most significant coordination jobs, and covers all five days. Attendees will also start asking for this to plan their travel, so advance the schedule as soon as you can.

From the *Local Chairs*, check **constraints** on rooms, coffee breaks, lunches, the P&D session, the gala, etc.  Then work with the *Doctoral Consortium Chairs*, the *Workshops and Tutorials Chairs* and the *Local Chairs* to figure out **room assignments for the first two days**.  Then figure out **keynotes, panel, P&D session, town hall** (typically the penultimate day), as well as slots for challenges, industry and main tracks.  Finally, coordinate across the main tracks to **group by topic and assign slots**, avoiding two or more similar topics in parallel.

> **TIP**: Try to keep the **same timeslots and session durations each day** so the "rhythm" for attendees is predictable — e.g. 1.5 hour sessions followed by a coffee or lunch break. Some exceptions are okay, but ideally minimal.

> **TIP**: This is a good time to ask the Main Track chairs to think about **award candidates**. As noted on the [OC page](organising-committee.html#selecting-the-best-papers), the presentations of award candidates should *not* be scheduled in the session immediately before the closing/awards ceremony — leave time for deliberation.

> **TIP**: Coordinate with **SWSA** on any awards they announce (Distinguished Dissertation, Test-of-Time/10-year). The recipients need to be invited with enough time to plan travel.
