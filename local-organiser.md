# The Local Organising Role

## Introduction

The *Local Chairs* are the team behind the original bid to host the conference. They are responsible for the **local delivery of the event**: the venue, accommodation guidance, travel advice, the social programme (welcome reception, banquet/gala, etc.) and the technical/AV infrastructure. They also work with the *General Chair* on the **financial planning of the conference**, including registration pricing and the allocation of complimentary and reduced registrations.

The *Web Presence and Publicity Chairs* traditionally report to the *Local Chairs*; details of their activities are covered in the [section on publicity](publicity.html).

This page collects the topics that the *Local Chairs* need to drive (often in close coordination with the *General Chair*).

---

## Time Line

### 18+ months — bidding and venue

The bid for the conference (including the venue and proposed dates) is submitted to [SWSA](https://swsa.semanticweb.org) typically two years in advance.  Once accepted, the *Local Chairs* should begin firming up venue contracts and rough capacity estimates.

### 12+ months — website and publicity

The website should be ready to launch at the previous year's ISWC (see the [Publicity section](publicity.html)).  Local information (city, travel, accommodation) should be on the site from the moment it goes live.

### 6–9 months — registration system and pricing

Open coordination with the *General Chair* on registration pricing, complimentary/reduced registration policy, and the registration platform.  Aim to **publish registration details at the same time as the main-track notifications** ([see Post-Notification Planning](post-notification.html)).

### 3–6 months — venue logistics

Finalise rooms (main track, workshops/tutorials, posters & demos, social spaces), AV, catering, dietary handling, signage, and student volunteer recruitment.

### At the conference

Be on hand to put out fires, manage the venue staff, AV team and student volunteers.  See [During the Conference](conference.html).

## The Venue

Coordinate with the *General Chair* and *Workshops and Tutorials Chairs* to check that the venue can accommodate the planned programme. Tricky details to check include:

- **room allocation** for workshops and tutorials (the number of parallel rooms caps how many workshops/tutorials can be accepted);
- **poster and demo space** (enough surface area, power, and standing room for the largest single session of the week);
- **catering flow** — enough tables and serving points to avoid queues during coffee breaks;
- **signage** — clear signs for which session is in which room, and how to get there;
- **AV** — how presenters connect, how slides are shown, screen sizes, recording infrastructure;
- **microphones** — for large sessions, four per room is a good target: one for the speaker, one for the chair, one for the current question, one for the next question (the chair can also manage without one);
- **session-chair desk** — somewhere for the chair to sit with their laptop and a timer.

> **TIP**: From experience, it is also worth checking with the venue that the conference is not double-booked with a wedding.

## Accommodation, Travel and Visas

Provide **travel advice and hotel suggestions** for attendees on the website from launch. Negotiate room blocks at nearby hotels where possible, and publish a range of price points (students need cheap options).

Set up a process to **generate invitation letters for visas** — many attendees will need them.  Ideally, these are generated automatically on registration.

---

## Social Events

The social programme typically includes:

- a **welcome reception** (usually on the evening of Day 1 of the workshops/tutorials);
- the **banquet / gala dinner** (alcohol and dancing are expected insofar as possible!);
- coffee breaks, lunches and any informal evening activities (city tour, etc.).

Check **dietary requirements**, including vegetarian and vegan options (make sure there's enough — carnivores eat that food too!) and allergens.

---

## Audio/Visual and Recording

Ensure that the AV provider is briefed on the format of each session type (keynote, main track, panel, posters & demos).  Decide early whether the conference will be **recorded**.  ISWC has typically worked with **Videolectures** (which SWSA tends to prefer); this needs to be **budgeted in** from the start.  Negotiations may cover:

- number of days of recording;
- number of parallel sessions to record (aim to record at least Main Track presentations, Keynotes and the Panel);
- number of personnel they will send;
- pre- and post-conference days they want on-site.

> **TIP**: Videolectures may request several days before / after the conference and a large team. **Negotiate**.

---

## Registration Pricing and Budget

The *Local Chairs* and *General Chair* set registration prices jointly. Prices should:

- cover venue, catering, AV, social events, recording, proceedings and other fixed costs;
- offer **student** and **early-bird** discounts;
- be **published at the same time as main-track notifications** so that attendees can budget their trip.

Coordinate with the *Sponsor Chairs* on the sponsorship contribution to the budget, and with the *Student Grants Chairs* on the call for student travel grants (which should go out **at or before the time of main-track notifications**).

It is important that the **quality of the technical programme is not compromised by "balancing the books"**; if the budget is tight, look for novel sponsorship opportunities and ways of encouraging registration rather than cutting programme quality.

## Free and Reduced Registrations

It is ultimately up to the *Local Chairs* and *General Chair* to decide who gets complimentary or reduced registration.  Some decisions are made closer to the time the registration opens, based on the projected budget. In recent years, the norm has been:

| Recipient | Registration |
| --- | --- |
| **All members of the Organising Committee** | Complimentary |
| **Workshop and tutorial organisers** | One or two complimentary or reduced per event |
| **Keynote speakers** | Complimentary |
| **Sponsors** | Number of complimentary registrations based on sponsorship level (see the sponsor packages from previous years) |
| **Other** | Avoid further free registrations; experience shows it is good to be **extremely stingy** unless the conference has unexpectedly strong sponsorship |

If you plan to change the current practice (e.g. no longer offer complimentary registration to OC members), **be explicit about it and tell OC members at the time of invitation**.

## Badges, Programme and Goody Bag

Check with the *General Chair* and *Web Presence and Publicity Chairs* what will be given to attendees on registration:

- a **printed programme** (something attendees at ISWC 2024 particularly appreciated);
- a **lanyard and badge** (decide what goes on the badge — name, affiliation, country, registration tier?);
- **wifi details** if needed;
- a **bag** — a nice tote bag is okay, but many attendees already have dozens of these, so keep it optional or skip it.

> **TIP**: Sponsor logos are usually printed on lanyards, programmes and signage. **Companies are picky about how their logos are used**: they want specific files, may insist on approval before publication, may ask for promotional material to accompany the logo, and tend to dislike logos on anything disposable. Check with them how their logo will be used!

---

## Student Volunteers

Recruit student volunteers early.  Brief them on:

- the layout of the venue and signage plan;
- session-chair support (timers, microphones);
- registration desk procedure;
- who their point of contact is on each day.

Avoid making any critical task depend on a single student volunteer.  If a volunteer has a key role, ensure they receive proper instructions or induction.

---

## Coordination with the Web Presence and Publicity Chairs

The *Web Presence and Publicity Chairs* normally report to the *Local Chairs* and are responsible for the website, social media, the call-for-papers campaign, on-the-day publicity, and recording activities. See the [Publicity section](publicity.html) for detail.

## Notes, Tips and useful Resources

* **TIP**: In ISWC 2024, the *Local Chairs* decided to hire a company to manage the website. This created significant delays as the company had certain policies and processes in place (e.g., 3 weeks to set up a custom domain). Be wary of this decision. Getting a couple of trustworthy, responsive PhDs or Postdocs to manage a WordPress instance or lightweight solution is usually a lot more agile.

* The Stefan Decker / `iswc<year>.semanticweb.org` DNS A-record process is described in the [Publicity section](publicity.html).
