# Roles and Responsibilities within the Organising Committee (OC)

## Introduction

One of the first things that needs to be done in the organisation of ISWC is the formation of the **Organising Committee (OC)**. Ideally, the *General Chair* should invite (and have accepted) chairs for each of the roles ***at least one year prior to the event*** (i.e. typically before the previous year's ISWC conference). This is typically done once the location (and the identity of the *Local Chairs*) has been finalised by [SWSA](https://swsa.semanticweb.org), so that invitees can confirm they can attend.

Traditionally, SWSA offers comments and advice on the key roles: primarily the chairs of the main tracks (Research, Resources, In-Use).  Therefore, when drawing up a shortlist, the *General Chair* should consult SWSA before reaching out to individuals.

**Diversity is important** for the OC (gender, geographical, etc.), so please be mindful of this.  It is also possible to check the names of previous chairs by consulting the [following spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/169mThBN6U09JvH_cILnV5rih2WIJz3kClfV4kXJeick/edit?gid=0#gid=0). Likewise, where possible, consider identifying a mix of individuals with experience together with those who have not served on previous ISWC OCs.

Note that in past years, some invitees may not respond promptly (or at all) to invitations, so factor in possible delays in the formation of the new OC.

### Sizing the Committee

It is a good idea to keep the OC **relatively small**. A smaller OC is easier to manage, and it reduces the number of complimentary conference registrations that need to be granted — which helps with the budget. Each time you consider increasing the OC from the previous year, ask whether **two roles could be combined** instead. Combinations that have been used in past years include:

- merging *Workshops* and *Tutorials* chairs;
- merging *Doctoral Consortium* and *Student Grants* (student fellowship) chairs.

The rule of thumb is: go over the OC carefully and ask whether each position truly needs to be separate.

### Conflicts of Interest

OC members **cannot submit papers to their own track**, and the *General Chair* cannot submit to any of the conference tracks.  *Workshops and Tutorials Chairs* cannot propose a workshop or tutorial; *Doctoral Consortium Chairs* cannot submit a DC paper. Even where the chair system allows recusal from the discussion of a specific submission, **the perception of unfair selection is hard to avoid**.

Mention this explicitly when inviting the corresponding chairs.

## Time Line

### 12-18 months

Aim to finalise the Chair positions at least one year before the prior ISWC Conference.  Ideally, the Website should be launched at that conference and list details of all members of the Organising Committee.

### 12 months (at prior ISWC Conference)

Ideally the *General Chair* (or if not possible, someone to represent the OC) should attend the previous year's ISWC to attend:

- the **Town Hall meeting** (to garner current feedback);
- the **OC Lunch** (traditionally on Day 3 of the main conference, where the main chairs of the prior and new ISWC can be introduced and exchange details);
- the **Closing Ceremony**, where a presentation of your event should be launched (typically with a video presentation) in coordination with SWSA and the prior *Local Chairs*.

Encourage your incoming track chairs to **set up video calls with their counterparts from the prior ISWC**. The OC Lunch has limited bandwidth for track-specific detail, and not every incoming chair will be at the conference; one-to-one handovers fill those gaps and produce the most useful tips.

### Handover

Typically, one week after the prior ISWC, the *General Chair* (or SWSA) should organise a **Handover** meeting with present and future ISWC Local and General Chairs. This allows the *General Chair* to introduce the new committee (so that they can coordinate the handover of materials), get into more detail on feedback, tips, material to hand over, etc., and set out some of the goals for the conference.  The meeting should start with the summary presentation of the prior ISWC conference (typically by the previous *General Chair* and *Local Chairs*), followed by a 5-10 minute talk by the new *General Chair*.

## Chair Positions and Responsibilities

Each of the chair positions is listed below.  Typically, two chairs are appointed for each role to ensure robustness and resilience; three is unusual but not unheard of. It is rare to have one chair — but if you do, avoid this on time-critical roles. The list of roles below reflects the main roles, but these can vary with each conference edition (for example, the inclusion of the *Job Fair Chair* in 2025 or the *Visionary Ideas Track Chair* in 2026).

### General Chair

Typically a single role — responsible for the overall management and delivery of the conference, including the appointment of the OC. Core management includes coordinating the overall programme of events (with the *Research Track Chairs*) and delivering the conference itself (with the *Local Chairs*). Also responsible (with the *Local Chairs*) for the financial planning of the conference.

| Associated Roles | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Sponsor Chairs** | Responsible for soliciting sponsorship (and subsequent advertising) for the conference, supporting the financial planning of the conference. Coordinate with the *General Chair*. |
| **Student Grants Chairs** | Oversee travel grants and other support for students at the conference. Coordinate with the *General Chair*. |

### Local Chairs

Responsible for the original bid to host the conference. These chairs coordinate the venue, accommodation guidance, travel advice, the social programme (welcome reception, banquet), AV and recording, and registration logistics. They also work with the *General Chair* on the **financial planning** of the conference, including registration pricing and the allocation of complimentary and reduced registrations.  They traditionally oversee the activities of the *Web Presence and Publicity Chairs*.

A dedicated page covers the local-organising role in detail: see the [Local Organisers](local-organiser.html) page.

| Associated Roles | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Web Presence and Publicity Chairs** | Manage updates to the web site and all publicity activities, including advertising calls, the event itself, activities during the conference, and recording conference activities. Typically report to the *Local Chairs*. |

### Research Track Chairs (a.k.a. *Program Chairs*)

The *Research Track Chairs* — referred to as *Program Chairs* in SWSA materials — are responsible for the **overall technical programme**. They directly run the **Research Track** and coordinate with the chairs of the other paper tracks (*In-Use Application*, *Industry*, *Resources*), but **not** the *Workshops and Tutorials Chairs* or the *Doctoral Consortium Chairs* (these report through the wider programme but are organised independently).

They manage the solicitation, review and selection of submissions for the main tracks, coordinate other events (such as invited talks, panels and the introduction of new tracks with the *General Chair*), and work with the rest of the OC to finalise the overall programme of the main and co-located events. They also run the **Senior Programme Committee** for the main tracks (see the [PC & SPC page](pc-and-spc.html)).

#### SWSA approval

**SWSA must approve the *Research Track Chairs***. The *General Chair* can invite candidates first (making the invitation conditional on SWSA approval), or get SWSA approval before inviting. Things to consider:

- there are usually **two** *Research Track Chairs*;
- they should preferably come from **different continents**;
- they should preferably have **different areas of expertise** in the Semantic Web field;
- there should preferably be **gender diversity** among the chairs;
- one — preferably both — should have prior **ISWC Research Track publications** before being nominated;
- they should have **served on SPC** before. Because the chairs for the upcoming conference are usually selected before the current year's SPC is announced, the *General Chair* may want to ask the current chairs to include the incoming chairs on the SPC to satisfy this requirement.

| Associated Roles | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **In-Use Application Track Chairs** | Responsible for managing the solicitation, review and selection of submissions for the In-Use Application track.  Coordinate with the *Research Track Chairs*. |
| **Industry Track Chairs** | Responsible for managing the solicitation, review and selection of submissions for the Industry track.  Coordinate with the *Research Track Chairs*. |
| **Poster, Demo & Lightning Talk Chairs** | Responsible for managing the solicitation, review and selection of demo submissions and poster publications (including late breaking submissions), together with main-track submissions that are accepted as posters.  Also responsible for coordinating the lightning talk session. Coordinate with the *Research Track Chairs*. |
| **Dagstuhl-style Workshop Chairs** | Responsible for managing the solicitation, review, selection and coordination of Dagstuhl-style workshops. These were introduced in ISWC 2025; although they are technically workshops, they are organised separately from the workshops submitted to the *Workshops and Tutorials Chairs* and may take place throughout the conference programme. |
| **Proceedings & Metadata Chairs** | Responsible for the coordination and assembly of the Proceedings Volumes (in coordination primarily with the *Research Track Chairs* and *General Chair*, but also with other track chairs where necessary). See [Proceedings](proceedings.html). |
| **Workshops and Tutorials Chairs** | Responsible for managing the solicitation, review and selection of workshop / tutorial submissions (forming the main co-located events that occur prior to the main conference programme).  Also responsible for coordinating with the successful individual tutorial and workshop organisers.  Need to coordinate with the *Local Chairs* to ensure capacity wrt rooms etc. |
| **Doctoral Consortium Chairs** | Responsible for solicitation, review, selection and running of the Doctoral Consortium. |

### Semantic Web Challenge Chairs

The **Semantic Web Challenge (SWC) Chairs** are the **only members of the OC that are not appointed by the *General Chair***. The SWC Chairs are appointed by the SWC steering committee and the current SWC chairs. The *General Chair* must approve the choice.

The SWC Chairs are responsible for identifying the topic of the *Semantic Web Challenge*, and for coordinating submission, review and notification of challenge submissions. Recently the challenge has had two tracks (the open track and the BTC track).

### Other Roles

Chair positions vary from year to year; here is a list of other roles that have existed in past years.

| Past Roles | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Panel Chairs** | Responsible for planning the panel session, including the selection of topics and speakers. |

## Keynote Speakers

Keynote speakers are typically invited by the *General Chair* in consultation with the *Research Track Chairs*. SWSA expects you to run candidate speakers by them before inviting.

There should be **gender and/or race diversity** among keynote speakers. If you are about to invite a third speaker and all three speakers would be white and male, contact SWSA for suggestions of other names. Try to balance industry/academia, core Semantic Web vs. related areas, geography and gender. Try to avoid inviting someone who has already given an ISWC keynote (or a recent ESWC keynote) unless there is a compelling reason.

[Lists of previous keynote speakers are given in this spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/169mThBN6U09JvH_cILnV5rih2WIJz3kClfV4kXJeick/edit?usp=sharing); please update it for future years.

Typically, the travel costs of keynote speakers will be covered (economy flights, registration, hotel), though you may want to double-check this with the *Local Chairs*. Note that *panel speaker* costs are not typically covered.

## Awards

Each year, ISWC presents a number of **awards**. Most awards have a sponsor; prior to the conference, the *General Chair* and *Sponsor Chairs* should ensure that there is a sponsor for each award.  It is the responsibility of the *Local Chairs* to **prepare the award certificates**, and payments usually go through them (unless noted otherwise below). Payments happen after the conference.

Except for the *SWSA 10-year award*, all awards are announced at the **Closing Ceremony**.

| Award | Decided by / Sponsor | Announced | Payment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SWSA 10-year award** | SWSA. Decided before the conference. | **Opening Ceremony** | SWSA directly (certificates by *Local Chairs*) |
| **SWSA Distinguished Dissertation Award** | SWSA. Decided before the conference. | **Opening Ceremony** | Travel reimbursement as for travel grants; award paid by SWSA. Certificates by *Local Chairs*. |
| **Best Paper** | *Research Track Chairs*, in consultation with the SPC. 3–4 nominations announced at the *Opening Ceremony*. | Closing Ceremony | *Local Chairs* |
| **Best Student Paper** | *Research Track Chairs*, in consultation with the SPC. Nominations announced at the *Opening Ceremony*. Papers nominated here may also be nominated for *Best Paper*; if a nominee wins *Best Paper*, another paper should be the *Best Student Paper*. | Closing Ceremony | *Local Chairs* |
| **Best In-Use Paper** | *In-Use Track Chairs*. Nominations announced at the *Opening Ceremony*. | Closing Ceremony | *Local Chairs* |
| **Best DC Paper** | *DC Chairs*. | Closing Ceremony | *Local Chairs* |
| **Best DC Mentor** | *DC participants*. First awarded in 2011. Optional. | Closing Ceremony | *Local Chairs* |
| **Best Poster** | *Poster & Demo Chairs* (in previous years, a large component was the vote of attendees at the poster and demo session). | Closing Ceremony | *Local Chairs* |
| **Best Demo** | *Poster & Demo Chairs* (as above). | Closing Ceremony | *Local Chairs* |
| **SWC Awards** | *SWC Chairs* (open track and BTC track in recent years). | Closing Ceremony | Arranged by *SWC Chairs* and sponsor |

Each OC may choose to add **new awards** (e.g. *Best Reviewer*, *Best SPC member*).

> **TIP**: Put together a [design for the award certificates](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/10LhO02k-xY54cG5e1I7ppzwdtoRGSPmP/edit?usp=drive_link&ouid=107368889730418788483&rtpof=true&sd=true) early and coordinate with the *Track Chairs* on signatures. If the relevant *Track Chairs* will not be attending, the *General Chair* can sign.

### Selecting the Best Papers

The *Research Track Chairs* manage this process. **Do not simply nominate the papers with the highest review score.** Approaches from previous years that have helped ensure fair selection include:

- asking SPC members to **nominate papers** for the awards;
- discussing nominations at the **SPC meeting**;
- having the *Research Track Chairs* **attend the talks** of all nominated papers before making the final decision.

> **TIP**: Award-candidate presentations should not be scheduled in the session immediately before the closing/awards ceremony — this gives time for deliberation.

> **TIP**: Coordinate with SWSA on any awards they announce (Distinguished Dissertation, 10-year/Test-of-Time). The recipients need to be invited with enough time to plan travel.

## Notes, Tips and useful Resources

* The [following spreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/169mThBN6U09JvH_cILnV5rih2WIJz3kClfV4kXJeick/edit?gid=0#gid=0) lists members of past Organising Committees and previous chairs — **please extend it for future years**.

* The *Research Track Chairs* should reach out to **previous conference chairs** for informal feedback on the best and worst PC and SPC members. SWSA does not maintain such lists. See the [PC & SPC page](pc-and-spc.html).

* **TIP**: People who are invited to the OC may not reply, or may say no, and the OC is large. Start the process **several months in advance** of the prior ISWC.
