# Conference Website and Publicity

## Introduction

The *Web Presence and Publicity Chairs* are responsible for the conference website, social-media presence, the call-for-papers advertising campaign, on-the-day publicity at the event, and the recording of conference activities. They traditionally report to the *Local Chairs* — see the [Local Organisers page](local-organiser.html).

The website is the **single most important publicity artefact** of the conference. It is the first place prospective authors look for CFPs, prospective attendees look for travel/accommodation guidance, and prospective sponsors look for the OC. **Get it up early.**

---

## Domain and Hosting

ISWC websites are served at `iswc<year>.semanticweb.org`.  The `semanticweb.org` domain is controlled by **Stefan Decker** (DERI); once the *Local Chairs* have an IP for the hosting, send it to Stefan to create the A-record. This address should be the **only conference URL** that is advertised.

---

## Time Line

### >12–15 months — prepare in private

Prepare the website with details of the event, location (including travel guidance) and the *Organising Committee*. Keep private until launch.

### 12 months — launch at the prior ISWC

**Immediately after the closing session of the previous year's conference**, launch / publish the website.  Initiate the publicity campaign — circulate the website link, event location, and dates.

### 6–9 months — CFPs and PC visibility

As each CFP is finalised, publish it on the website and run a coordinated push on social media and the relevant mailing lists. Publish the PC list on the website as soon as members have had a chance to update their name/affiliation/country.

### 2–3 months — programme details

In the couple of months leading up to the conference, the website needs to fill out with details of accepted papers, the PC, the full programme, social events, and travel/accommodation details. The Web Chairs will be **busy** during this window — brief them early so they can plan capacity.

> **TIP**: With more details fixed (keynotes, panels, etc.), this is also a good time to encourage **local attendees** to attend. A nice-looking brochure with the conference's key selling points, sent on local mailing lists, is an effective approach.

### During and after the conference

Social-media coverage of keynotes, awards and the town hall; post-conference publication of slides/videos where available.

## Mailing Lists for the CFP Campaign

The campaign should target the right mailing lists and venues. See SWSA's [mailing lists for conference announcements](http://swsa.semanticweb.org/content/mailing-lists-conference-announcements) for the canonical starting list, and supplement with discipline-adjacent lists where they fit the year's scope (NLP, KR, databases, etc., depending on the call).

---

## Notes, Tips and useful Resources

* **TIP**: Getting the website, domain, logo, banner, etc., ready can take time, so this process should be started **several months prior to the previous ISWC**. The website is crucial and getting it up early is very *very* important!

* **TIP**: In ISWC 2024, the *Local Chairs* decided to hire a company to manage the website. This created significant delays, as the company had certain policies and processes in place (e.g., 3 weeks to set up a custom domain). Be wary of this decision. Getting a couple of trustworthy, responsive PhDs or Postdocs to manage a WordPress instance or lightweight solution is usually a lot more agile.

* The *Local Chairs* should have the homepage up with the OC and conference dates **before the prior ISWC**.

* [SWSA mailing lists for conference announcements](http://swsa.semanticweb.org/content/mailing-lists-conference-announcements).
