# The Programme Committee and Senior Programme Committee

## Introduction

ISWC operates a **two-tiered programme committee** for the main tracks:

- a **regular Programme Committee (PC)** of approximately 100–150 reviewers;
- a **Senior Programme Committee (SPC)** of approximately 20 senior researchers in the field.

The *Research Track Chairs* (also referred to as *Program Chairs* in SWSA materials — see the [Organising Committee](organising-committee.html) section) select both committees, with the goal of ensuring the highest possible quality of reviews and decisions.

Given the size of the regular PC, it is hard to ensure uniformity of review: across a hundred-plus reviewers there will inevitably be differences in professionalism, ability and thoroughness. The SPC exists to address this. Each SPC member is responsible for a sizable portion of the conference papers, and acts as the integrator across the reviews assigned to those papers.

This page focuses on the **people and process** side of reviewing. For the *configuration* of the review system itself (EasyChair, review forms, decision options), see [Managing EasyChair](Easychair-CFP.html).

---

## Role of the SPC

SPC members **act as associate editors of a journal would**. For each paper assigned to them, an SPC member:

- looks at all reviews and the author rebuttal (if available);
- produces a **meta-review and recommendation**;
- requests additional reviews if the existing ones are insufficient;
- looks at the available reviews **critically**, rather than simply averaging the recommendations.

SPC members can **overturn the recommendation of reviewers** if they feel it was inappropriate, provided they can give a reasoned and well-justified decision. The expectation is that SPC members are proactive and involved in the decision, able to defend the acceptance or rejection of each paper in their pile.

> The *Research Track Chairs* should make these expectations explicit when inviting SPC members, and throughout the process.

---

## SPC Membership

When selecting the SPC, the *Research Track Chairs* should ensure that, between them, the SPC members **cover the full range of topics in the CFP**.

Experience shows that it is good to keep a balance: a fair number of SPC members who have already served in this role, together with a sizable contingent of **new SPC members** each year.

> **TIP**: SPC has been called *Vice-Chairs* or *meta-reviewers* in previous years. *Meta-reviewers* has been eschewed for several years now; either *SPC* or *Vice-Chairs* is fine, but be consistent.

---

## The SPC Meeting

The *Research Track Chairs* must organise a **physical or virtual SPC meeting** at which all SPC members discuss the papers before making the final decisions. SPC members must prepare their meta-reviews and recommendations **before** the meeting.

It is up to the *Research Track Chairs* to decide how to run the meeting, but a good rule of thumb from previous years is to discuss (at least briefly) **every paper that received at least one "accept" recommendation** either from a reviewer or from an SPC member. This ensures that no paper falls through the cracks.

### Setting the meeting date *before* SPC invitations go out

This is important: the **SPC meeting date should be fixed before SPC members are invited**.  By agreeing to join the SPC, a member commits to attending the meeting on the designated day.  Reserving the date in advance also avoids the trap of trying to find a slot across twenty senior researchers' diaries after the fact.

---

## Inviting the PC and SPC

When recruiting reviewers, the *Research Track Chairs* should:

- include the **submission and notification dates** with the invitations so invitees know what they are committing to;
- consider **inviting the SPC before the PC**, as the tracks tend to recruit from overlapping pools and an SPC commitment can crowd out a PC role;
- coordinate across tracks, since the same researchers may be invited by multiple tracks (Research, Resources, In-Use, Industry) and may accept one role and then reject another;
- give the PC a chance to **edit/update their name, affiliation and country** before the list is published on the conference website.

Once the PC is set, **publish the list on the website** — early visibility helps encourage submissions.

---

## Black and White Lists

SWSA does **not** maintain black lists of reviewers or SPC members. However, the *Research Track Chairs* should **reach out to previous conference chairs** to get their feedback on the best and worst PC and SPC members. This informal handover is one of the most valuable things the OC handover meeting (see the [OC page](organising-committee.html#handover)) can deliver.

---

## No Self-Submission

OC members **cannot submit papers to their own track**, and the *General Chair* cannot submit to any of the conference tracks. This rule also applies to the *Workshops and Tutorials Chairs* (no workshop / tutorial proposals) and the *Doctoral Consortium Chairs* (no DC submissions).

Even if there are technical means to recuse from the discussion of a specific paper, **the perception of unfair selection is hard to avoid**. The *General Chair* should mention this rule explicitly when inviting the corresponding chairs.

See also the [Organising Committee](organising-committee.html) page.

---

## Notes, Tips and useful Resources

* **TIP**: Make the SPC meeting commitment **non-negotiable** at the point of invitation. The single biggest source of late-stage problems is an SPC member who turns out not to be able to attend the meeting.

* **TIP**: The *Research Track Chairs* should give SPC members **clear, written guidance on the decision process** at the start of the reviewing window, not at the end. SPC members tend to follow the framing they receive early.

* Make good use of **previous years' review forms** and modify only where necessary. See [Managing EasyChair](Easychair-CFP.html) for the mechanics.
